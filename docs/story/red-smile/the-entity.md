# The entity

**Status: established (2026-08-19).** It moves through media — radio, television,
computers — and it uses **images** to transfer itself into a host. It **kills
society agents** because it feels threatened by them — and it does that killing
through the hosts it takes, which it inoculates and keeps as **vessels**. Everything
under *Proposed* below extrapolates from that; the two paragraphs under *What it
is* are canon.

> **It has no name.** Possibly it should not get one — a thing that lives in
> transmission is more frightening as "it", and the society calling it anything
> at all implies they understand it. If a name is needed for captions, the
> society's working label is better than a true name: *the signal*, *the
> passenger*, *the picture*.

## What it is

Not a ghost and not a demon in the classical sense. It has no body and no place.
It exists **in the medium** — it travels through radio, television, computers,
anything that carries a transmission — and it crosses from the medium into a
person by way of an **image**.

The image is the vector. Not the sound, not the words, not the wiring: **the
picture**. To see it is to be exposed. That is the entire mechanism, and it is
why the whole series is drawn rather than filmed.

## What it looks like

It has no appearance of its own, so it wears a shape: the **unicursal hexagram**
— one continuous unbroken interlaced line, taller than wide, elongated vertical
axis, a small lens-shaped void at its centre. That is not a symbol *about* it.
It is what it looks like when it is on a surface. A self-portrait, and a lure.

## Episode 1 is the infection, panel by panel

Read page 1 of `redsmile-static` again with this in hand. It is not a prologue —
it is the transmission:

## It knows it is being hunted

**Established:** the entity kills [society](the-society.md) agents. Not hosts
them — kills them. It does this because it **feels threatened**, which settles
the largest question this file used to carry: it is not weather and it is not a
process. It has self-preservation, it can tell the difference between a person
who is looking at it and a person who is looking *for* it, and it acts on that.

It has no hands. So it makes them.

**Established:** [Elena](elena.md) and [Marcus](marcus.md) are inoculated as
**vessels** — hosts kept, carried and used, whose purpose is to kill. That closes
the circle: the entity cannot cross a room, but a person can, and a person who
still has a face, a name and a security pass can walk right up to someone.

| Who | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| A civilian | **Taken and inoculated.** The image crosses, the sigil surfaces in the iris — and what is grown in them is a weapon |
| A society agent | **Killed.** Not hosted, not crossed into. Removed — and removed *by a vessel*, not by anything with a shape |

So the two behaviours are one mechanism. It takes civilians in order to have
something to kill agents *with*. Every host is ammunition, and every agent who
dies is killed by a person who was somebody's neighbour a fortnight ago.

The smile, then, is not the residue of a person being eaten. It is what a
finished weapon looks like.

### What that costs

- **It has had ninety years of practice.** Everything the society knows dates
  from 1934 at the earliest. The entity does not.
- **The agent tier is attrition.** Placed agents are ordinary people in ordinary
  jobs, unprotected and deniable, and the thing they watch for kills them
  specifically. [Elena's mother](elena.md) worked that tier. So does
  [Halina](halina.md), tonight, alone in a building full of screens.
- **Cover is not tradecraft, it is armour.** Halina's cleaning contract is the
  only thing standing between her and a category the entity handles differently.
  The panel where it works out what she is, is the panel where she stops being
  safe — and the reader can be ahead of her on it.
- **The knife is someone she knows.** Marcus is a vessel and Halina cleans his
  building at night. The most obvious end of that thread is the chief executive
  walking down a dark floor toward the woman with the mop, perfectly pleasant,
  in a very good suit.
- **It explains why there are only three at the top.** Not selectivity. Losses.
- **It cannot be provoked cheaply.** Anyone who moves against it openly is
  telling it what they are.
- **You cannot fight it directly, only its people.** Every confrontation is with
  a victim. Tokiro ends things in the room — and the thing in the room will be
  wearing a face, and will not have chosen any of this.

### The obvious question the story must not answer too early

If it kills agents on sight, why is Halina still alive? Either it has not
identified her — the cover is working — or it has, and it is waiting to see who
she reports to. The second is much worse and should be available without ever
being confirmed.

## Proposed — what a host becomes

The title is the answer. A host ends as **the smile**: the closing plate of
episode 1, teeth in the dark, the person gone from behind them.

That gives the series its clock. Between the iris and the smile there is a
person still walking around, still answering the phone, still being seen by
people who love them — and the society's whole job is the hours in between.

## 1934 — it gets into television

**Established.** The entity was in the heads of the **first television users, in
1934**. Not a later arrival to the medium: it was there for the earliest
audiences, when a viewer was a rarity and a receiver was a piece of laboratory
equipment in somebody's front room.

That is the moment it stopped being rare. Before television it moved by any image
that could be reproduced and looked at — printed in the corner of a playbill, cut
into a shutter, painted on a sign. Slow, local, one pair of eyes at a time. A
broadcast image is the same vector at a scale nothing before it could offer: one
picture, many rooms, simultaneously, and nobody has to go anywhere to see it.

Every decade since has handed it a wider aperture. Television, then colour, then
screens in every room, then screens in every pocket, then screens that choose
what to show you. [Marcus](marcus.md) runs the current one.

**This is why [Viktor](viktor.md) matters and why he founded
[Sub Signo](the-society.md).** He was there when it took the first audiences.
He is the only living thing that can say *this is the same one* — everyone else
inherits the claim.

## Proposed — why the hunters wear its mark

Viktor and Adaeze both carry the unicursal hexagram tattooed on their skin. If
the sigil is the entity's transfer vector, wearing it deliberately is either
suicidal or the whole trick.

Proposal: it is **inoculation and anchor**. A banisher marks himself with the
thing's own shape so that the image has nowhere new to arrive — the aperture is
already occupied — and so that he can look at it directly and act. It is
protective equipment.

Tokiro refuses it, and now his refusal is a real argument rather than a quirk:
he will not carry the thing's picture on his body, and he goes into rooms
unprotected because of it.

## Proposed — how to write it

- **Never draw it as a creature.** No face, no figure, no silhouette. It appears
  only ever as an image on a surface: a screen, a window, a wet road, a mirror,
  a photograph. The moment it has a body it stops being frightening.
- **The surface is the horror.** A dead television is a threat. So is a phone
  face down on a table, because it can be turned over.
- **Sound is the tell, the image is the harm.** Radio can carry it, but radio
  alone cannot complete a transfer — it can only bring it near. That asymmetry
  is useful: a scene can be tense without being fatal.
- **Do not let the society explain it fully.** Viktor should be able to say what
  it does and be unable to say what it is.

### The one to be careful with

The reader is looking at drawn images on a screen. The series can point at that,
and it will be effective exactly once. Spend it deliberately — as the end of an
episode, never as a running gag — and never in a way that makes the reader feel
tricked rather than caught.

## Open questions

1. Does a vessel know? Is there anything of Elena left inside the thing that
   used to be her, and can it be reached?
2. Can a vessel be disarmed short of killing it — and has the society ever
   managed it?
2. Can a host be saved after the iris but before the smile — and is that what
   the society actually does?
3. What happened to Elena? Episode 1 ends on the smile. Was she the first case
   the three arrive too late for, or the one they get?
4. Can it be *ended*, or only ever cut off from a medium?
5. Was Adaeze's father one of its hosts, or an unrelated demon?
6. How does it identify an agent? By what they do, what they carry, or who they
   have spoken to? The answer decides how Halina gets caught.
7. Do the wards work? Viktor and Adaeze wear its sigil as protection — but they
   are also the two it has most reason to kill.
