# Sub Signo

**Status: established (2026-08-19).** The three are members of a secret society
that fights dark entities, and it is called **Sub Signo**. Everything beyond
that is a pitch.

> **Sub Signo** — Latin, *under the sign*. The society is named for the mark its
> members carry, not for its absence:
>
> - **Under the sign.** [Viktor](viktor.md) and [Adaeze](adaeze.md) wear the
>   unicursal hexagram on their skin — the entity's own shape, worn deliberately
>   as ward and anchor. They work beneath it, in both senses: protected by it and
>   subject to it.
> - **Under the standards.** *Sub signis* is the Latin for troops serving
>   together under their unit's colours. It is the oldest possible way to say
>   "these people are one body", and it dignifies three people and a cleaner
>   without pretending they are an institution.
>
> **[Tokiro](tokiro.md) is the exception that proves it.** He is *sub signo* by
> membership and refuses to be *sub signo* on his body. He will not carry the
> thing's picture on his skin, and goes into rooms unprotected because of it. The
> name says what the society is; he is the standing argument about what that
> should cost.
>
> **Latin, so it stays Latin.** A proper noun in all four caption languages —
> never *Sotto il Segno*, *Unter dem Zeichen* or *Sous le Signe*. Pronounce it
> *soob SEEG-noh*; check it on the first generated line, and spell it
> phonetically in the TTS input only if ElevenLabs mangles it.
>
> The name sounds older than the society is. Sub Signo was founded in the
> **1940s** — it is not an ancient order. But it was named by a man born in the
> 1670s, and Latin is simply what he reached for. The organisation is younger
> than television; its founder is older than the Enlightenment.

## What it is

**Founded by [Viktor](viktor.md) in the 1940s**, after he escaped an attack by
the thing it now hunts. It is not an inherited order and there is nobody above
him — Sub Signo is one survivor's answer to what he watched happen in Berlin in
1934, when [the entity](the-entity.md) got into the heads of the first
television audiences.

**Three principals, and people underneath them.** The three are Sub Signo at its
top — not its entire headcount. The qualification for the top is unteachable:
Viktor has three and a half centuries of recognising things, Adaeze can feel
them, Tokiro can end them. Nobody is promoted into that.

Below them sit placed agents: ordinary people in ordinary jobs, positioned where
the society needs eyes. [Halina](halina.md) is one — a cleaner on a night
contract inside [Marcus's company](marcus.md), which tells you the three already
suspect the company, or at least its screens.

| | Member | Role | Not human |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scholar | [Viktor](viktor.md) | Occultist, master banisher — knows what a thing *is* | Half vampire, 350 |
| Finder | [Adaeze](adaeze.md) | Field operator — locates and reaches | Half demon |
| Blade | [Tokiro](tokiro.md) | Akuma banisher — ends it in the room | Fully human |

**Two of the three are partly the thing they hunt, and the one who is not is the
one who does the killing.** That is the top of the society in one line, and every
scene between them should be aware of it.

The tier split does real work. The three are irreplaceable and cannot be
replicated; the agents are ordinary, deniable, and **unprotected**. Viktor and
Adaeze wear the unicursal hexagram as a ward. Halina has no mark at all —
either nobody equips a junior, or a ward would blow the cover. She is in the most
exposed position in the story with the least protection in it.

It also keeps the series' shape intact: **each episode is a host.** Being on the
payroll is not immunity, and an agent can be taken as easily as a civilian — more
easily, because she is the one sent toward it.

And the tier has a history. **Elena's mother worked it too**, which means the
society has been losing these people for a while, and that the cost of the work
does not stop at the person doing it.

Worse: [the entity](the-entity.md) kills agents **using vessels** — civilians it
has taken and pointed. So the society's dead are killed by the very people it
exists to save, and every rescue is a disarmament.

## The mark

Viktor and Adaeze both carry the **unicursal hexagram** (see [README](README.md)).
Tokiro refuses it.

The obvious question the story has to answer: **that is the same sigil that fills
the television on episode 1 page 1 and surfaces in Elena's iris.** If the
hunters wear the entity's own mark, it needs a reason.

The proposal: a banisher's tattoo is a **ward and an anchor** — you mark yourself
with the sigil of the thing so you can stand in front of it and act on it. It is
protective equipment, not allegiance. Tokiro's refusal then becomes an actual
argument rather than a quirk: he thinks a banisher who wears the sigil has
already conceded something, and Viktor thinks it is only a tool.

If you would rather the hunters' mark be a *different* symbol from the entity's,
say so — it changes both sheets and the compact prose locks.

## What this replaced

Earlier drafts in this folder had Viktor as the antagonist who broadcast the mark
at Elena, Adaeze as his colleague in that order, and Tokiro as their opposition.
**All three are wrong** and have been rewritten. They are on the same side.

The antagonist role that vacated is now filled — see
[the-entity.md](the-entity.md). The three are the people who respond to it, not
its authors, and episode 1 page 1 is the infection rather than a prologue.
