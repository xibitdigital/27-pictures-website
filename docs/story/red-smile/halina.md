## Appearance — locked

Matches the character sheet prompt. Binding.

A white Eastern European woman, **mid 50s**, on a corporate cleaning contract.

**Robust.** Broad through the shoulders and hips, heavy-set and physically
strong — a body built by thirty years of work. Not fat, not slim: solidly
powerful. Average height, which puts her a head below Marcus in any shared panel.

Broad face, high cheekbones, weathered skin, no makeup, deep lines at the mouth
and between the brows. **Tired eyes that miss nothing** — the whole character is
in that combination.

**Long hair**, dark going grey, thick, scraped back into a low practical bun with
loose strands escaping at the temples. Long enough that it clearly reaches past
her shoulders when it is down — worth showing down at least once, because it
changes her completely and the story should keep that in reserve.

**Costume.** A corporate cleaning uniform: plain dark short-sleeved tunic polo, a
knee-length utility tabard over it with a large front pocket, loose dark work
trousers, flat rubber-soled shoes. An ID badge on a plain lanyard at her chest —
**blank**, no lettering, because baked text cannot be translated. Blue
disposable gloves on one hand only, the other bare.

**No occult mark.**

## Proposed — why she is there

She is **placed**. The cleaning contract is a cover and [Sub Signo](the-society.md) put her in
[Marcus's building](marcus.md) on purpose, which means the three already suspect
the company — or at least suspect its screens.

The cover is close to perfect, and not because it is clever. It is perfect
because it is *true*: she really does the job, every night, properly, for the
wage. There is nothing to catch her at. And nobody in that building has ever
looked at her long enough to wonder.

**Not a senior agent.** She is not one of the three. She does not banish
anything, she is not briefed on everything, and she may not know what the sigil
actually does — only that she is to note where it appears and tell no one. That
gap matters: she can be in more danger than she understands, and the reader can
know it before she does.

What she is very good at is the thing she was hired for: thirty years of other
people's offices. What is on the desks after hours, who is still in the building,
which room has been locked since Tuesday, which monitor was off yesterday and is
on tonight. That is intelligence no surveillance budget buys.

## Proposed — the job title, both ways

"The cleaner" has a second sense in fiction — the person who arrives after a mess
to make it never have happened. The reveal that she is an agent makes that pun
live, and the discipline is **not to press it**.

She is not a killer and not a fixer. She is a watcher with a mop who is genuinely
good at both halves. If the series ever needs her to be more than that, it should
cost her the cover permanently — that is a one-time expenditure, not a mode.

## Proposed — exposure

Being an agent does not make her safe. It arguably makes her worse off:

- [The entity](the-entity.md) **kills agents** — it does not take them. And it
  does the killing with **vessels**: civilians it has already taken. Being
  briefed is not armour.
- **[Marcus](marcus.md) is a vessel, and she cleans his building at night.**
  That is how it ends: the chief executive walking down a dark floor toward the
  woman with the mop, perfectly pleasant, in a very good suit. **Episode 2.**
- She carries **no mark**. Viktor and Adaeze wear the unicursal hexagram as a
  ward; Tokiro refuses it on principle. Halina does not have one at all — nobody
  gave a junior agent protective equipment, or it would blow the cover.
- If she is taken, the society loses its only position inside the company **and**
  one of its own, and Marcus will never know either happened.

That is the version worth writing: the most exposed person in the story turns out
to be on the payroll, and it has not helped her.

## Established — how she dies

**Marcus kills her in episode 2.** The first time the series shows what a vessel
is actually *for*, and it is not a monster in a doorway — it is a man she has
seen in the lift, in his own building, at an hour when nobody else is in it.

Everything already in place pays off at once:

- The entity kills agents, and cannot do it itself. Marcus is the hand.
- Her cover was her only armour, and cover fails silently — she will not know it
  has failed until he is already walking toward her.
- She carries **no mark**. Viktor and Adaeze have wards. Nobody equips a junior.
- Nobody in the building knows her name. That is tradecraft on Monday and an
  epitaph on Tuesday.

**Play it quiet.** He should not transform, roar or reveal anything. He should be
courteous, because he is always courteous, and the horror is that the courtesy
does not stop. She may well greet him first.

**The society finds out immediately, and still too late.** [Viktor](viktor.md)
feels it — episode 2 closes on him, somewhere else, knowing. He comes. Nothing
about that helps her, and Marcus chairs a meeting the next morning.

## Proposed — how to use her

- **Night interiors.** Corridors, lifts, dark open-plan floors lit by monitors.
  She and Marcus share a building and should almost never share a panel.
- **Play her as a civilian first.** The reader should meet a tired woman doing a
  hard job. The agent reveal is worth far more after they have worried about her
  as a bystander — and her death is worth more again after that.
- **Competent, unbothered, and now readable twice.** A woman who has worked
  nights for decades is not jumpy — and on a second read, every time she notices
  something she was noticing it *professionally*.
- **Hair down = off duty = out of cover.** One deliberate visual lever, used once.

## Open questions

1. Who recruited her, and how long has she been in that building?
2. Which of the three is her handler? Adaeze finds things; Tokiro pulled someone
   out once before. Either answer characterises them.
3. How much has she been told? Does she know what the sigil does, or only that
   it matters?
4. Has she already seen it — and did she know to look away?
5. Does she recognise what Marcus is before the end, or not at all?
6. Who is the first person in the story to use her name — and is it said over
   her body?
7. Who was at home for her? Elena's episode turned on being alone; hers should
   turn on someone waiting.
