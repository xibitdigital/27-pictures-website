## Appearance — locked

Matches the character sheet prompt. Binding.

A white English man, **late 30s**. **Tall**, upright, lean and fit — the build of
a man who has never worked outdoors and is not yet tired. Clean shaven. Long
even-featured face, strong straight nose, clean defined jawline, smooth
unweathered skin, no lines at the eyes.

**Classic English haircut:** short back and sides, neat side parting, dark and
full, no grey, combed and controlled.

His youth must read as **confidence, never inexperience**. The bearing is the
same either way: composed, unhurried, entirely at ease — a man used to being the
youngest person in every room he runs.

**Eyeglasses.** Slim rectangular frames, thin dark rims, worn in **every** view
including the back view and the silhouette. The glass stays clear and never
opaque — his eyes must read through the lenses. A blanked-out lens is a horror
shorthand this character has not earned yet, and spending it early wastes it.

**Costume.** An excellent contemporary business suit: dark, two-piece,
single-breasted, sharply tailored, crisp white shirt, plain dark tie, polished
black oxfords. Discreet cufflinks, a small plain lapel pin.

**No overcoat, in any view.** The other three all wear long coats — Viktor's
Victorian wool, Tokiro's haori, Adaeze's charcoal knee-length. Marcus does not,
and the absence is the characterisation: he is never outdoors, never in weather,
never in the field. The world is brought to him.

**No occult mark** visible anywhere.

## Proposed — which company

The obvious and best answer: **the company is the medium.** A broadcaster, a
platform, a device maker — whatever it is, its screens are in the rooms where
[the entity](the-entity.md) arrives, and its business is putting images in front
of people at scale.

**He is a victim, not an author.** Not complicit, not an arrangement, not feeding
anything on purpose. And it went further than that: **he has been taken.**

He is a **vessel** — inoculated, kept, and pointed. Which makes the company
answer bleakly funny: the entity did not need to corrupt the man who owns the
screens. It only needed him to look at one.

What remains open is not whether, but **when and how visible**:

- **How far along is he?** Between the iris and the smile there is a man still
  chairing meetings, still persuasive, still recognisably himself to everyone who
  works for him. That is where he is most useful to the story and most dangerous
  to everyone in the building.
- **Does he know?** A vessel that knows and cannot stop is a different, worse
  character from one that does not. Neither is redeemable by the end.
- **Who is he for?** A vessel exists to kill agents, and there is one in his
  building. **He kills [Halina](halina.md) in episode 2** — courteously, in his
  own tower, at an hour when nobody else is in it. He does not transform and does
  not reveal anything; the courtesy simply does not stop.

Do not show the reader the moment he turns. He is far more frightening as a courteous man in a good suit whom the reader already knows is loaded.

## Proposed — how to use him

- **The society is already inside his building.** [Halina](halina.md) cleans his
  offices on a night contract and reports to them. He does not know, and the
  reader can. Every scene in that tower is played over that gap.
- **Power is not protection.** He is the only character with power in the
  ordinary sense, and it buys him nothing: the entity does not care about
  lawyers. That is the point of him.
- **He is the only character with power in the ordinary sense.** Viktor has
  centuries, Adaeze has blood, Tokiro has a sword — Marcus has lawyers, a
  building and a press office, and against those the society is three people
  with no standing.
- **He is the youngest man in the story and controls the widest aperture.** A
  350-year-old occultist and a thirty-eight-year-old chief executive, and it is
  the young one whose reach is total. Put them in a room together and let the
  age gap do the work without either of them mentioning it.
- **Never let him be crude.** He should be courteous, well briefed and genuinely
  persuasive. If he sneers, he is a lesser character — and a vessel that acts
  sinister is a vessel that has stopped being useful to the thing driving it.
- **His smile is a loaded object.** In a series called RED SMILE, where the
  terminal state of a host is a grin in the dark, drawing this man's broad warm
  public smile is a deliberate act. The head sheet carries it on purpose. Use it
  once, late, and let the reader make the connection.
- **Interiors only.** Offices, studios, glass, screens. He should never be drawn
  in a street or in weather.

Direction: warm, articulate, expensively educated English — but a younger voice
than the old sheet implies. The register of a man used to being listened to and
never having to raise his voice for it. Eleven v3,
moderate-to-high stability — his control is the point. **Never** the `ai`/`badai`

himself.

## Open questions

1. What is the company, and what does it actually sell?
2. Does he know what he is, and how long has he been it?
3. Has the society approached him, and what happened?
4. Is the lapel pin just a lapel pin?
5. Did he found the company or inherit it? At this age it matters — a founder is
   culpable for the design, an heir only for the running of it.
